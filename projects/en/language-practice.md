# language-practice

## One-Line Summary

An English learning desktop app that brings media import, transcription, playback, practice, and AI interaction into one workspace.

## Problem

The most common break in language learning is not a missing feature. It is a broken flow. You use one tool to listen, another to read captions, a third for shadowing, and then jump again to ask about grammar or phrasing. The whole process keeps getting interrupted.

That fragmentation looks harmless at first. In practice it keeps taxing attention. The problem is not that people cannot learn. It is that they cannot sustain a full sequence of learning actions without friction pulling them apart.

## Why This Direction

You can always improve one stage in isolation. Build a better player. Build a better vocabulary view. Build a better AI tutor. Each one can make sense on its own. But learning outcomes often stall because the transitions between those actions are too expensive.

So I did not keep building single-purpose tools. I focused on continuity instead, so import, comprehension, practice, and feedback can happen in the same local environment. Once the flow holds together, refinement at the feature level becomes much more valuable.

## Quality Bar / What I Care About

I care most about smooth switching, low interaction cost, stable local behavior, and a learning loop that people can return to and continue without rebuilding context every time.

A good learning tool should not ask users to reconstruct the setup at every step. It should let them pick up where they left off and keep going.

## Engineering Judgment

This project reflects a view I have about learning products: effectiveness does not come from stacking more features. It comes from reorganizing fractured actions into a natural flow.

What often looks like a willpower problem is really a workflow design problem.
